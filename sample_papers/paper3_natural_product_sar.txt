Enantioselective Total Synthesis and Antifungal Evaluation of Lycopodine-Derived Alkaloids
Claire E. Beauchamp, Kenji Takahashi, and Robert F. Alvarez
Laboratory for Synthetic Chemistry and Pharmacognosy
DOI: 10.1021/acs.jnatprod.2026.1105

Abstract:
A scalable asymmetric total synthesis of the tetracyclic alkaloid derivative (+)-Lycopodine Analogue 6b has been achieved in 11 linear steps from commercial cyclopentenone. The critical C-C bond forming cascade leverages an intramolecular organocatalytic Michael-aldol cyclization with >99:1 enantiomeric ratio. In vitro biological profiling against pathogenic fungal strains identified Compound 6b as a selective inhibitor of Candida albicans biofilm formation, displaying a minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of 1.8 µg/mL. Deprotection conditions utilizing trifluoroacetic acid (TFA) in dichloromethane at 0 °C afforded the target alkaloid in 84% yield without epimerization.

Introduction:
Lycopodium alkaloids present intricate polycyclic architectures decorated with contiguous stereocenters. Natural isolates frequently exhibit neuroprotective, antimicrobial, and immunosuppressive properties. Accessing non-natural synthetic analogues enables systematic alteration of steric bulk and hydrogen-bonding motifs to dissect structure-activity profiles.

Synthetic Sequence and Asymmetric Construction:
The synthesis initiated with an enantioselective conjugate addition catalyzed by a chiral secondary amine catalyst (10 mol %). The key tricyclic ketone intermediate 5 was assembled through a microwave-assisted cascade sequence in toluene at 110 °C.

Scheme 2 highlights the final deprotection and functionalization:
Intermediate 5b was treated with trifluoroacetic acid (TFA, 20% v/v) in anhydrous CH2Cl2 at 0 °C for 45 min under a nitrogen atmosphere. Neutralization with saturated NaHCO3 followed by extraction with dichloromethane afforded (+)-Lycopodine Analogue 6b in 84% isolated yield.

Antifungal Activity and Cellular Selectivity:
Synthetic analogues 6a–6d were screened against fungal pathogens Candida albicans (ATCC 90028) and Aspergillus fumigatus (AF293). Amphotericin B served as the positive clinical benchmark.

Table 1: Antifungal Minimum Inhibitory Concentrations (MIC, µg/mL)
Alkaloid Analogue | Candida albicans MIC | Aspergillus fumigatus MIC | Cytotoxicity IC50 (Vero Cells)
Compound 6a       | 14.5 µg/mL           | >64 µg/mL                 | >100 µM
Compound 6b       | 1.8 µg/mL            | 28.4 µg/mL                | >100 µM
Compound 6c       | 8.2 µg/mL            | >64 µg/mL                 | 75.2 µM
Amphotericin B    | 0.5 µg/mL            | 1.0 µg/mL                 | 12.5 µM

Compound 6b demonstrated potent, selective inhibition of C. albicans with an MIC of 1.8 µg/mL and favorable therapeutic index (>55-fold selectivity over mammalian Vero cells). 

Notice on Scope:
The present manuscript focuses exclusively on in vitro antifungal potency and synthetic methodology. In vivo pharmacokinetic evaluation (e.g., rat oral bioavailability or clearance) and single-crystal X-ray crystallographic coordinates were not investigated in this study.
